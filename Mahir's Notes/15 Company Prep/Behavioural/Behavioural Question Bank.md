# Behavioural Question Bank

> The questions that actually recur. Map each to a story in your bank, and check for gaps.

## Ownership and Initiative

- Tell me about a time you took on something outside your job scope.
- Describe a time you saw a problem nobody owned and fixed it.
- Tell me about a project you drove from start to finish.
- When did you sacrifice a short-term win for a long-term outcome?
- Tell me about a time you had to make a decision without your manager.

## Failure and Learning

- **Tell me about your biggest professional failure.**
- Describe a time you made a mistake that affected customers.
- Tell me about a time you received difficult feedback.
- When did you have to admit you were wrong?
- Tell me about a decision you'd make differently now.

**These are the highest-value questions to prepare.** A candid failure story with a concrete lesson scores very well; deflection scores very badly.

## Conflict and Influence

- **Tell me about a disagreement with a colleague or manager.**
- Describe a time you convinced someone who initially disagreed.
- Tell me about working with a difficult person.
- When did you have to influence without authority?
- Tell me about a time you were overruled. **What did you do next?**

The last one is the Amazon "Disagree and Commit" probe. **Answer both halves.**

## Technical Depth

- **What's the hardest technical problem you've solved?**
- Describe a system you designed end to end.
- Tell me about a production incident you debugged.
- When did you have to dig several levels deeper than expected to find a root cause?
- Describe a significant trade-off you made and why.
- Tell me about technical debt you took on deliberately.

Expect 3–4 levels of follow-up. **Know your numbers.**

## Delivery Under Pressure

- Tell me about a time you had to deliver under a tight deadline.
- Describe a time you had to cut scope. How did you choose?
- When did you have to say no?
- Tell me about competing priorities and how you resolved them.
- Describe a time you had to work with incomplete requirements.

## Learning and Curiosity

- Tell me about something you learned recently and applied.
- Describe a time you had to become productive in an unfamiliar technology quickly.
- How do you keep current?
- Tell me about a time you sought out a problem outside your comfort zone.

## Customer Focus

- Tell me about a time you improved the customer experience.
- Describe a time you pushed back on a requirement because it was wrong for users.
- When did customer feedback change your approach?
- Tell me about a time you had to balance customer needs against engineering constraints.

## Collaboration and Mentorship

- Tell me about mentoring someone.
- Describe a code review where you disagreed with the author.
- When did you help a struggling teammate?
- Tell me about improving how your team works.

## Standards and Quality

- Tell me about a time you refused to ship something.
- Describe a time you raised the bar on quality.
- When did you push back on a "good enough" solution?
- Tell me about a process you improved.

## Questions To Ask Them

Prepare 3–5. Weak questions are a real negative signal.

**Good:**
- What does success look like in this role at six months?
- What's the most significant technical challenge the team faces right now?
- How does the team handle on-call and incident response?
- How are technical decisions made when engineers disagree?
- What's something you'd change about how the team works?

**Avoid:** anything answerable from the job posting, and compensation questions during technical rounds.

**"What would you change?" is the strongest question in the list** — it elicits an honest answer and tells you what you'd actually be walking into.

## Coverage Check

Before the loop, confirm you have a story for each:

- [ ] Biggest failure
- [ ] Conflict with a colleague
- [ ] Overruled — and committed anyway
- [ ] Hardest technical problem
- [ ] Delivered under deadline / cut scope
- [ ] Learned something new fast
- [ ] Influenced without authority
- [ ] Went beyond your scope
- [ ] Ambiguous requirements
- [ ] Data changed your mind

**Any unchecked box is a question you will be asked and cannot answer well.**

## Revision Summary

The question set is small and predictable. Map 8–12 stories across these categories, verify no category is uncovered, and rehearse aloud with a timer. Prepare questions to ask — weak ones count against you.

## Quick Recall

- Failure, conflict, and overruled are the three most-avoided and most-weighted
- "Overruled" must include the commit half
- Technical depth questions go 3–4 levels — know your numbers
- Prepare 3–5 questions to ask
- Rehearse **aloud**, timed at 2 minutes
