# STAR Method

## Why It Matters

Behavioural rounds are pass/fail at Amazon and heavily weighted everywhere else. They're also the cheapest round to prepare for — the questions are known in advance.

## The Structure

| Part | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | 15% | Context — just enough to make the problem legible |
| **Task** | 10% | **Your** specific responsibility |
| **Action** | **60%** | What **you** did, step by step |
| **Result** | 15% | Quantified outcome + what you learned |

**Target 2 minutes.** Under 90 seconds is thin; over 3 minutes loses the interviewer.

## The Three Failures

### 1. "We" instead of "I"
The most common and most damaging. The interviewer is assessing *you*, not your team.

> ✗ "We migrated the service and reduced latency by 40%."
> ✓ "I proposed the migration, benchmarked three approaches, and owned the rollout. Latency dropped 40%."

Credit the team once, then speak in first person.

### 2. Too much Situation
Candidates spend 90 seconds on background and 20 on their actions. **Invert it.** The interviewer needs only enough context to understand the difficulty.

### 3. No numbers
"Improved performance" is unscoreable. "Reduced p99 from 800 ms to 120 ms, cutting timeout errors from 3% to 0.1%" is evidence.

**If you don't have exact figures, estimate and say you're estimating.** That's fine, and far better than vagueness.

## Building The Story Bank

You need **8–10 stories**, not 30. Each should be reusable across several questions.

Cover these situations:

| # | Story type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hardest technical problem you solved |
| 2 | A significant failure and what changed afterwards |
| 3 | Conflict with a colleague or manager |
| 4 | Disagreed with a decision, and what happened |
| 5 | Delivered under a tight deadline / made a trade-off |
| 6 | Learned something new quickly and applied it |
| 7 | Influenced without authority |
| 8 | Improved something nobody asked you to improve |
| 9 | Handled ambiguity or unclear requirements |
| 10 | Data changed your mind |

**Stories 2, 3, and 4 are the ones candidates avoid and interviewers weight most.** A prepared failure story with a genuine lesson is a strong positive; "I can't think of a real failure" is a strong negative.

## Story Template

```
TITLE:        (one line, so you can find it under pressure)
LPs / traits: (which competencies it demonstrates)

SITUATION:    2 sentences. What system, what scale, what was at stake.
TASK:         1 sentence. What YOU specifically owned.
ACTION:       4–6 bullets. Decisions, trade-offs, why you chose each.
RESULT:       Numbers. Business impact, not just technical.
LEARNING:     What you'd do differently, or what you now do routinely.
```

**The Action section should contain trade-offs, not just steps.** "I chose X over Y because Z" is what demonstrates engineering judgement. A list of tasks completed does not.

## Handling Follow-Ups

Interviewers probe. Be ready for:

- "What would you do differently?"
- "How did you measure that?"
- "What did others think of your approach?"
- "What was the hardest part?"
- "What was the trade-off you accepted?"
- "How did you convince them?"

**Prepare answers to these for your top 5 stories.** The follow-ups are where the round is actually decided — the initial story just opens the topic.

## Failure Stories — How To Tell One

1. Choose a **real** failure with real consequences
2. Own it fully — no blaming a teammate, vendor, or manager
3. Explain what you did to contain it
4. State the concrete change you made afterwards
5. Show the change stuck (a process, a test, a check that still exists)

**Don't use a disguised humblebrag** ("I work too hard"). Interviewers recognise it instantly and it reads as evasive.

## Common Mistakes

- "We" throughout
- 90 seconds of setup
- No quantified result
- The same story reused for four questions in one loop
- A story from more than ~4 years ago when recent examples exist
- Rambling — practise aloud with a timer, not silently
- Blaming others in the conflict or failure story

## Revision Summary

Situation and Task briefly, Action at 60%, Result quantified. Speak in first person. Build 8–10 reusable stories including failure, conflict, and disagreement. Prepare the follow-ups for your top five — that's where the round is decided.

## Quick Recall

- 15/10/60/15 split, ~2 minutes
- **"I", not "we"**
- Numbers in every Result
- 8–10 stories, each reusable
- Failure, conflict, and disagreement stories are mandatory
- Prepare follow-ups, not just the story
